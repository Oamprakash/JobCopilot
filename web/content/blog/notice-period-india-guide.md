In India, the notice period often matters as much as your skills. Many IT companies ask for 60 or 90 days, plenty of product companies would rather hire someone who can join in 30, and a long notice can quietly move you down a recruiter's list. Here's how to handle it honestly at each step.

*This is practical experience, not legal advice. Your employment contract is what actually applies to you.*

## Before you apply: know your real number

Open your appointment letter or contract and find three things:

1. **Your notice period** - and whether it changed when you were promoted or confirmed. Some companies have a shorter notice during probation.
2. **Whether a buyout is allowed** - some employers let you pay salary in lieu of the remaining notice; others don't, or leave it to their discretion.
3. **Whether unused leave can be adjusted** against notice. Policies differ a lot, so check rather than assume.

Knowing these before the first recruiter call means you can answer clearly instead of guessing.

## When an application or recruiter asks

Answer with your contractual notice, plus anything that genuinely shortens it:

> "My notice period is 90 days. A buyout is possible, and I'd expect to be able to join in about 45."

Don't say 30 days if it's 90. It's the most common thing candidates stretch, and it tends to come out at the offer stage, when it can cost you the offer.

If you're already serving notice, say so, with your last working day. Many recruiters actively look for people who can join soon.

## When you get an offer

- **Ask whether they'll support a buyout.** Some companies reimburse part or all of a notice buyout for the right candidate, often as a joining bonus. It doesn't hurt to ask once, politely.
- **Get the joining date in writing** in the offer letter before you resign.
- **Ask your current manager about early release** once you've resigned. Many will agree if you plan a proper handover. Offer a clear handover plan; it's the strongest argument you have.

## Before you resign

- Have the signed offer, not just a verbal yes.
- Resign in writing and keep a copy of the acknowledgement.
- Finish your handover properly. You'll need a **relieving letter** and **experience letter** for the next company's background check, and a smooth exit makes those easy.
