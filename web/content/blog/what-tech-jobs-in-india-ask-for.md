Every job advert is a small list of what a team wants. Put a few hundred of them side by side and you get something more useful than any "top skills" article: what companies hiring in India are asking for *this month*.

The numbers below come straight from the open jobs TrulyApply reads from companies' own career sites (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and similar boards). They update by themselves every few hours, so this page is never out of date. Agencies and duplicate re-posts aren't in it, which keeps it closer to what real teams want.

A few things to keep in mind when you read this:

- **A mention isn't a requirement.** Plenty of adverts list a skill under "nice to have". Read the job itself before you decide you don't fit.
- **Common doesn't mean valuable.** Git and SQL show up everywhere because almost every team uses them. They're expected, not a reason to be shortlisted.
- **The gaps matter more than the totals.** If a skill appears in a large share of jobs for your role and isn't anywhere on your resume, that's the one to fix: either you have it and haven't said so, or it's worth learning next.

If you're an Android engineer, compare this with the skills section of your own resume. The usual mismatch I see is not missing skills but missing *words*: people who've shipped Compose screens for two years and never write "Jetpack Compose" anywhere, so a recruiter scanning for it moves on.

## How to use this for your next application

1. Pick the three skills that appear most often for your role.
2. For each one you genuinely have, make sure it's in a bullet that shows what you did with it, not only in a list at the bottom.
3. For each one you don't have, be honest. Don't add it. Either learn it or apply to jobs that don't ask for it.

If you want to see this for a single job rather than the whole market, [check your resume against a real job](/#try). It shows which of the job's skills your resume covers and which it doesn't, in about a minute.
